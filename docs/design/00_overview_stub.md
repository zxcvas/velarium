# Design: Overview

Living high-level design. M1 is the playable console slice.

## Core Fantasy
You are a **lanista** in Capua: owner of a *familia gladiatoria*, barred from honour (*infamia*), not from profit. You train men, lease them to editors, and — if the ludus earns a name — you may one day stage a *munus* yourself.

The game is **Amphiteater**. The velarium is only the awning over the cavea. You live in its shade, not in the curia.

## Player Role
- **Now:** lanista. Buy, feed, drill, and rent gladiators.
- **Later (unlock):** *editor* for an afternoon. Same sand, your purse on the line, *mitte* / *iugula* in your hand.
- You do not start as a magistrate. Hosting is progression, not the opening toy.

## Setting
Capua, early Principate (a.u.c. DCCLXXXII). The city still remembers Spartacus. Pompeii, Puteoli, Nola, Neapolis are a day's rumor away. Names are Roman: *tria nomina* for the player; inscription-style single names for the familia (Flamma, Celadus, Spiculus…).

## Major Pillars
1. Gladiators as property, athletes, and tombstones (record: *pugn. / vic. / stans / mis.*)
2. The show as the day's risk — but you *rent* to it before you *give* it
3. Roman society as a locked door: infamia, aediles, duumviri
4. The Gaius contract: little for sweat, much if they die — and then you have no man

## M1 Slice (what is playable)
Day loop in a console ludus:

- Inspect the familia
- Orders: palus / sparring with *rudes* / requies
- Forum: buy, medicus, rumors
- **Locatio:** an editor wants a type; terms *pro sudore* vs *occisus*
- Combat text, classic pairings (murmillo–thraex, retiarius–secutor)
- **Edere munus:** locked until fama 16 and a palma in the house
- End day: upkeep, training, events, Roman calendar

Failure: empty cells and an empty purse. There is no scripted "win"; hosting your first munus is the slice's payoff.

## Non-Goals (M1)
Godot, art, politics tree, gladiatrices, imperial ludi, save-scumming UI, multiple cities as maps.
