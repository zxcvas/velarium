## A. Primary texts (systems)

### Gaius, *Institutiones* 3.146

Latin (standard text): *pro sudore denarii XX* if the man leaves *integer*; *denarii mille* if *occisus aut debilitatus*. The jurists treat the first as *locatio conductio* (hire), the second as *emptio venditio* (sale).

Edition: *Gai Institutiones*, tr. Edward Poste, 4th ed. Whittuck, with intro. by A. H. J. Greenidge (Oxford). Also in Justinianic discussion of hire/sale.

**Use:** spine of the lanista economy.

### Pliny the Elder, *Naturalis Historia* 18.14 (72)

Loeb LCL 371. Gladiators *hordearii vocabantur* (“barley-men”). Barley as oldest grain, tied to that nickname.

**Use:** kitchen / diet.

### Pliny the Elder, *Naturalis Historia* 36.203

Quotes Varro: drink lye from hearth ashes for bruises; gladiators helped by this after combat.

**Use:** medicus “ash drink” upgrade. Support for Ephesus Sr/Ca, not standalone proof.

### Petronius, *Satyricon* 117

Gladiator’s oath: to be burned, bound, beaten, and killed by the sword (*uri, vinciri, verberari, ferroque necari*).

**Use:** *auctoratus* recruitment text.

### Juvenal, *Satura* 6 (Oxford fragment / nearby lines on the ludus)

The lanista quarters net-men apart from armored heavies and convicts — a “cleaner house” than the satirist’s target.

**Use:** mixing *armaturae* in cells → quarrel events.

### Suetonius, *Divus Augustus* 45

Augustus restricted *sine missione* (no-reprieve) fights.

**Use:** 29 CE lethality toward Ville’s 1st-c. band, not 3rd-c. slaughter.

### ILS 5113 — Flamma

*Secutor*, Syrian, lived 30 years; fought 34, won 21, *stans* 9, *missus* 4.

**Use:** inscription UI template (`pugn. / vic. / stans / mis.`).

### CIL IV 4342, 4345 — Celadus

Pompeii graffiti: Celadus the *thraex*, *suspirium puellarum*; short unbeaten record.

**Use:** fama / crowd, not a romance sim.

### Pompeii *edicta munerum* (CIL IV; programs)

Name the editor, pair counts, sometimes a *velarium*.

**Use:** locatio flavor and hosted-munus posters.

### Pompeii armor stamps NER / NER. AUG

From the theatre-quadriporticus barracks: gear of the *Neroniani*, imperial school at Capua.

**Use:** Capua as the gladiator city; player is *not* the imperial procurator.

## B. Archaeology (base)

### Luciana Jacobelli, *Gladiators at Pompeii*

L’Erma di Bretschneider, 2003; English tr. Mary Becker. Review: BMCR 2005.07.59.

After the earthquake of 62 CE the theatre *quadriporticus* became a ludus: cells ~10–15 m² on two levels, straw not beds, 2–3 men; kitchen and dining; graffiti on columns; helmets, greaves, *galerus*. Jeweled female skeleton in a cell — guest or lover; do not over-read.

### D. L. Bomgardner, *The Story of the Roman Amphitheatre*

Routledge, 2000. Pompeii barracks: courtyard area close to the amphitheatre’s arena; one easily guarded entrance; capacity estimates on the order of 100–200.

### Ludus Magnus (Rome)

Excavated 1937 and 1957–61. Amanda Claridge, *Rome: An Oxford Archaeological Guide*. Parco archeologico del Colosseo: Domitianic school, ellipse ~62 × 41 m, cells on the portico, linked to the Flavian amphitheatre; *saniarium* / *armamentaria* in the quarter.

**Date warning:** Flavian — after a.u.c. DCCLXXXII. Late-game image, not the tutorial map.

## C. Osteology and medicine (body)

### Lösch et al. 2014

Sandra Lösch, Negahnaz Moghaddam, Karl Grossschmidt, Daniele U. Risser, Fabian Kanz. “Stable Isotope and Trace Element Studies on Gladiators and Contemporary Romans from Ephesus (Turkey, 2nd and 3rd Ct. AD) — Implications for Differences in Diet.” *PLOS ONE* 9(10): e110489. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0110489](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0110489).

22 gladiators vs civilians: C3 staples (wheat/barley), relatively low δ¹⁵N (legumes), **significantly higher Sr/Ca** (plant-ash calcium source).

**Date warning:** 2nd–3rd c. Ephesus, not Capua 29 CE. Diet *type* is usable; do not copy the cemetery as a map.

### Kanz & Grossschmidt 2009

Fabian Kanz & Karl Grossschmidt. “Dying in the Arena: the Osseous Evidence from Ephesian Gladiators.” In T. Wilmott (ed.), *Roman Amphitheatres and Spectacula* (BAR IS 1946), 211–220.

Trauma patterns; limited “overkill” vs medieval battlefields.

### Galen at Pergamum (c. 157–161)

Physician to the high priest’s gladiators. Wound care (oil, wine-soaked linen); criticizes bean-mash + barley *sagina* meant to pad the body. Secondary: J. Scarborough, “Galen and the Gladiators,” *Episteme* 5 (1971); V. Boudon-Millot; Loeb *On the Constitution of the Art of Medicine* introduction.

**Date warning:** method and medicus *role*, not an NPC in 29 CE.
